### Methods, data and evidence

MAP-ExPLoc asks which sequence properties drive a protein-localization predictor and whether its attributions agree with independently annotated localization determinants. SHAP explains a specified model/reference game; annotation overlap is evidence about model behavior, not experimental confirmation or a causal mechanism. [Execution instructions](research-software.md) cover the framework and the separate human predictor study.

#### Predictors and attribution

Inputs are protein sequences or FASTA: case/whitespace are normalized; empty sequences, gaps and residues outside the standard 20 are rejected. The studies use canonical sequences; unsupported biology, including selenoproteins, is consequently excluded.

The served Random Forest (RF) uses 423 descriptors: length, 20 amino-acid frequencies, 400 overlapping dipeptide frequencies divided by `max(length−1, 1)`, GRAVY and Biopython isoelectric point. These describe whole sequences. A dipeptide-frequency attribution cannot be assigned to particular occurrences. TreeSHAP explains class probabilities using fitted tree-path counts as its reference. Only verified RF/Extra Trees routes with traceable transformed-feature identities are supported; opaque projections and margin outputs are rejected. Complete signed contributions must reconstruct the served probabilities within `1e-6`.

[DeepLoc 2.1](https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/services/DeepLoc-2.1/) has ten multilabel outputs, distinct from the RF's five mutually exclusive labels. Reports retain native class order, mode-specific thresholds, strict threshold comparison and the four-decimal nearest-threshold fallback when no class passes. Scores are not normalized across compartments. Fast uses ESM1b (10–1,022 residues); Accurate uses ProtT5-XL-UniRef50 (10–4,000). MAP-ExPLoc rejects longer sequences instead of applying native middle truncation. Accurate requires single-sequence batches because native pooling includes padding. Native attention and sorting-signal outputs are not SHAP values.

The default **region KernelSHAP** game partitions the first/last 50 residues into ten-residue windows, with up to six interior regions; proteins shorter than 100 residues use contiguous ten-residue windows. Partitions do not depend on annotations. For each protein, four deterministic whole-sequence shuffles supply fixed references. Absent regions are replaced from each reference, and complete predictor probabilities are averaged across the resulting sequences; altered sequences receive fresh preprocessing/embeddings. KernelSHAP uses an identity link, no feature-selection regularization and 512 sampled nontrivial coalitions, enumerating all when fewer exist. References preserve length/composition, but hybrid coalitions can change composition; both destroy sequence context and can be outside the natural distribution. Values attribute **regions, not individual residues**.

Reports retain exact references, seeds, coordinates, all classes, base values and contributions. The full coalition equals native prediction; reconstruction tolerance is `1e-5`. Additivity does not establish convergence or biological faithfulness. Historical sensitivity checks vary coalition seed/budget while keeping references fixed. Schema-v3 diagnostics additionally distinguish reference sampling, methodological sensitivity and intervention effects. Warning thresholds are absolute attribution change >0.02, magnitude-rank Spearman correlation <0.8, sign agreement <0.9 for contributions >0.005 in magnitude, or top-20%-region Jaccard overlap <0.6; constant explanations are uninformative. These are operational thresholds, not calibrated biological confidence.

Global summaries are class-specific mean absolute and signed contributions. Region summaries first average within each protein's terminal/interior category, then across proteins; matching coordinates do not establish alignment. Schema v3 adds equal-group summaries, medians, 10% trimmed means, 1,000-replicate group-bootstrap intervals and separately named attribution density. Group counts and coverage accompany results; fewer than 20 groups trigger a warning. These summaries are exploratory unless tied to a prespecified analysis.

Positional overlays require the exact annotated sequence/hash, accession/isoform, source/version, evidence and original coordinates. Internal intervals are zero-based half-open; displayed positions are one-based inclusive. Overlaps remain separate annotations; uncertain boundaries are excluded from exact-coordinate statistics. Sequence mismatches are rejected without inferred alignment. Missing annotation does not mean absence of a biological signal.

#### Biological evidence and current qualification

The historical experiment in `examples/validation/v2/` selected 30 accession-ordered sequence-group representatives from 730 eligible reviewed eukaryotic proteins in a 9,450-record UniProt snapshot retrieved 2026-09-18. Eligibility required 100–250 residues and an experimentally supported, precisely bounded signal peptide starting at residue 1 and ending at 20–40. Evidence had to support the positional feature itself. MMseqs2 connected components used ≥30% identity and ≥80% bidirectional coverage.

For DeepLoc Fast's Extracellular output, the estimand was positive SHAP per residue in complete signal-contained windows minus matched nonsignal windows within the first 50 residues, averaged equally across groups. All 30 proteins had eligible controls. With 1,000 group bootstraps, the contrast was **0.009415** probability units/residue (95% interval 0.006857–0.012581). However, 1,000 annotation–attribution pair randomizations within length/terminal strata yielded mean 0.009174 and **one-sided p = 0.077922**: no support beyond the matched null. Twenty-eight signals occupied the same first two complete windows, limiting null discrimination. One of three sensitivity checks exceeded the 0.02 threshold; the other 27 proteins were unassessed. Ten of 30 exactly matched available localization/sorting-signal supervision; homologous and foundation-model exposure were not excluded. The raw contrast interval is not evidence against the matched null. `protocol.json`, `biological-validation.json` and `supervision-overlap.json` contain the definitions, full null distribution and overlap audit.

The newer `examples/validation/v2x/` bundle is **partial**. Fast passed native full-precision parity (`4.77e-7` maximum probability difference; tolerance `1e-5`), rounded-CSV parity (`5.1e-5` tolerance) and decision agreement on the saved panel. Capacity tests on an M2 Max/32 GB Mac used MPS/float32, lengths 10–1,022 and batches 1/4/16; these measure computation, not biological accuracy. Accurate assets were verified, but loading stopped at memory preflight (10.9 GiB available versus a conservative 20 GiB estimate). Accurate inference/parity, both-mode method comparison and final biological evaluation have **not** been completed. CUDA and mixed precision are unqualified. The two-protein Fast workflow report is not the planned development benchmark.

New cohorts derive from 75,023 reviewed eukaryotic records retrieved 2026-09-19. Precise feature-level experimental evidence is required. Joint sequence clustering precedes development/reference-pool/evaluation splitting; historical groups are excluded. Available localization, HPA/test, sorting-signal and membrane datasets are audited for accession, exact-sequence and global/local similarity; unavailable mappings remain unknown. Foundation-model exposure remains unknown.

The frozen protocol compares whole shuffles, 20-residue block shuffles and positional amino-acid backgrounds on development proteins, followed by five-/ten-residue versus legacy partitions. Selection must use stability and independently drawn, length-preserving interventions against matched low-attribution/random regions, never historical/final annotation overlap. A replacement requires ≥90% informative comparisons stable and a positive paired group-bootstrap lower bound for faithfulness improvement in **both** modes; otherwise retain the legacy method. No replacement has been selected.

Formal biological families require ≥60 independent groups after coordinate/control exclusions and ≥80% planned power for standardized effect 0.5 under twelve planned family/mode hypotheses. Only secretion and mitochondrial targeting currently exceed the group minimum after coordinate screening; power/null checks remain pending. NLS and plasma-membrane helices are underpowered after exclusions; PTS1/ER-retention cohorts are too small and require matched sequence controls rather than terminal overlap. Final analyses prescribe 1,000 randomizations/bootstrap draws and Holm correction. Cohort membership, family-specific outputs, exclusions and unresolved eligibility are authoritative in `v2x/cohorts/protocol.json` and `v2x/coordinate-feasibility.json`; they are not biological results.

#### Human predictor study

This study predicts **one supported structured annotation**, not exclusive localization. Both human source downloads contain the same 20,431 reviewed UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot records, release 2026_03, retrieved 2026-09-18; removing class caps did not supply an independent release. Curation requires a canonical sequence, one mapped class and `ECO:0000269`attached to a mapped location. Protein-existence evidence alone is insufficient.

| Cytoplasm     | Cytoplasm, Cytosol             |                  316 |
| Membrane      | Cell membrane, Plasma membrane |                  406 |
| Mitochondrion | Mitochondrion                  |                  154 |
| Nucleus       | Nucleus                        |                  677 |
| Secreted      | Secreted                       |                  188 |

Unmapped/conflicting structured locations and isoform/product-specific comments exclude records. Identical sequences are deduplicated; conflicting duplicate labels are excluded. Missing localization is not negative or imputed. Free-text notes do not determine acceptance and can describe additional compartments. Curation accepts 2,107 proteins (10.31%); exclusions and evidence are in `examples/experiments/research-revision/audit/`.

The historical baseline capped each class at 500 (seed 42), yielding 1,458 training and 364 holdout proteins. Expanded development excludes those 364 accessions and two related proteins, leaving 1,741 proteins/1,510 sequence groups. MMseqs2 18-8cc5c uses ≥30% identity/≥80% bidirectional coverage and connected components over original plus accepted expanded sequences; rejected records are not bridge nodes. No qualifying development–historical hits remained. This does not exclude domain/remote homology or shared experimental dependence.

Selection uses three outer stratified group folds, repeated with seeds 20260918/20260919, and three inner folds. Scaling, model selection and temperature fitting stay inside training folds; the original RF is refitted on identical outer rows. Fifteen candidates span a prior dummy, length-only logistic, L2 multinomial logistic on 23/63/423 features and four forests. The 23-feature representation omits dipeptides; 63 adds first/last-50-residue composition (whole sequence if shorter; overlapping termini allowed). Linear models use `log1p(length)` and training-fitted standardization. Selection chooses the least complex candidate within 0.01 macro-F1, 0.02 log loss and 0.05 per-class F1 of the leader. `primary/protocol.json` records candidates and complexity ranks; remaining ties prefer smaller C, then candidate ID.

Primary estimands are adaptive-selection minus matched-RF macro-F1, and calibrated minus raw adaptive-selection log loss. Metrics pool out-of-fold predictions across repeats; macro-F1 includes all five classes, assigning zero to undefined class F1. Paired bootstrap resamples sequence groups, retaining both repeats: 2,000 draws, seed 20260918, 97.5% intervals for two primary comparisons. Intervals condition on fitted predictions; repeated predictions are not independent samples.

The adaptive macro-F1 gain is **0.0632 (97.5% interval 0.0357–0.0907)**. Five outer folds selected terminal forests and one terminal logistic. Full-development selection chose 63-feature logistic, C=0.1; its row is a post-selection diagnostic, not independent validation of the final artifact. An exploratory exact-model calibration check failed its retention gate (≥0.02 log-loss reduction and interval excluding zero), so the saved final temperature is 1. That check and stronger label permutations were added after earlier results were inspected. The served RF remains the separate historical 128-tree artifact (holdout macro-F1 0.5223), not this logistic model.

The adaptive gain persisted in publication-linked and note-free sensitivity analyses; neither is independent confirmation. Development includes 313 proteins with localization notes; 67 supporting publications overlap development and historical-related records. Publication grouping controls only recorded links. Cytoplasm recall for the final logistic configuration is 0.3212; confident errors remain. Selection for experimental annotations and baseline class caps prevents proteome-wide prevalence/accuracy claims. Tissue, condition, assay and fragment metadata are incomplete; assayed mature proteins may differ from canonical precursors. The five-class predictor has no validated multilabel, out-of-scope or abstention behavior. Other species, isoforms and fragments are outside its evaluated scope. All reported human-model performance is internal; neither independent population transfer nor experimental localization has been established.
